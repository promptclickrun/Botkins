Botkins on iPhone Duo

One resizable iOS app, two displays, six poses. Closed, Botkins is the phone app you know with its bars moved to the side rail. Open, it shows one more level of hierarchy: the chat list beside the chat, the chat beside what the bot changed. Half-folded, it turns into a remote control for the bot's computer. Specs and rules come from Apple's Designing for iPhone Duo HIG. Sizes are drawn in points at 1× (outer 466×678 pt, inner 890×626 pt).

1 · The side rail, and what Botkins puts on it

On the outer display, and on the inner display in landscape, the system moves the status bar, toolbar and tab bar into one vertical rail on the trailing edge, aligned under the camera. That rail is the "weird side menu". The trick is to treat it as two stacks: navigation and actions from the top, tabs from the bottom, and let overflow eat the middle.

Rail order, top to bottom

  1. Camera and Dynamic Island. Always present. Botkins' existing Live Activity (the working bot head) expands down the rail here, not across the top.
  2. Status bar. System-owned. Stacked, not rotated.
  3. Back / Close. Always first. HIG: primary navigation sits at the top of the rail.
  4. Prominent action. New chat, New task, Send to bot. Filled, accent color. Highest visibility priority so it never overflows.
  5. Remaining toolbar group. Model, Context, People. Symbol plus title for every item; titles appear in the overflow menu.
  6. Overflow (…). System menu only. Botkins' own "…" menus move into it, so there is one ellipsis.
  7. Tab bar. Home, Chats, Work, Market, Settings. Badges stay visible longest because they carry status ("Needs you").

Items overflow bottom-to-top by default. We set priority so Back, the prominent action and badged tabs survive.

Compression policy per screen

ScreenKindWhen space runs out
HomeNavigationToolbar into …, keep tab bar
ChatsNavigationToolbar into …, keep tab bar

Text buttons ("Done", "Review") cannot go vertical. Botkins uses symbols on the rail and keeps text-only actions in the content.

2 · Closed: the outer display

Wider and shorter than any other iPhone (1.45:1). Compact width, so one pane. The rail gives back the top and bottom bars, which is roughly two extra chat messages of height.

Closed · portrait

Home

  • The custom top row ("Demo Mac" pill, round + button) goes away: the computer becomes a menu beside the title, + becomes the rail's prominent action.
  • Bot list and Today keep full width. Nothing in content is inset for a bottom bar any more.
  • Work badge stays on the rail tab.
Closed · portrait

Chat, bot working

  • Task-oriented: tab bar minimizes to one control, the toolbar keeps Back, Model, Context, People.
  • Patch's Live Activity grows down the rail under the camera. Tap opens the turn timeline.
  • Composer stays in content, horizontal. Send stays next to the text it sends; it is not a rail action.
Closed · landscape

Chat in landscape

  • Only 466 pt of rail. Tab bar minimized, Context and People drop into the overflow menu; Back and Model stay.
  • The rail follows the camera, so it is drawn on the edge the camera lands on. Which edge that is depends on rotation direction. Verify in Xcode Device Hub before building.
  • Messages get a readable 60-character measure instead of a full-width line.

3 · Open: the inner display

Regular width. Add exactly one level of hierarchy, the Mail pattern: closed shows the list or the chat, open shows both. Same tabs, same actions, same state.

Open · landscape

Chats list beside the chat

  • Rail stays on the trailing edge, same order as closed (People moves to overflow at this height), so muscle memory carries over when you unfold.
  • List controls (Search, Filter, New) sit above the leading pane because they act on the list. The rail only holds controls for the chat. That is the HIG's "controls near the content they affect" rule.
  • Built on NavigationSplitView semantics: collapses back to one pane when closed, keeps the selected chat.
  • Inner camera is under the display and hidden (dashed circle). Nothing reserves space for it until it turns on.
Open · portrait

Home with horizontal bars

  • The one pose where the rail goes away: standard top and bottom bars come back. Same five tabs in the same order.
  • Team grid uses 4 columns and Today uses 2. Even column counts split cleanly at the fold.
  • Today panels get room for the rich HTML panels they already render.

4 · Half-folded poses

The fold becomes a reserved region. Keep taps and key text out of the hatched band, give each half one job, and change as little as possible on the way from flat to folded.

Book · landscape, partly folded

Chat on the left, what the bot changed on the right

  • Panes snap to equal widths when folded (Notes pattern), then relax to list-plus-detail when flat.
  • Right half is the existing Changes / artifact view: the diff Patch made, with Approve on the rail. Reading a diff while you talk about it is the best use of two pages.
  • Built with an arrangement view (split) inside the chat screen. Navigation stays outside it, per the HIG.
Best new useLaptop · portrait, folded flat on a table

Remote screen: watch a bot drive your computer

  • Top half is a display, angled toward you: the live Screen view of the paired Mac or VM. No taps up there.
  • Bottom half is the control surface, flat on the table: trackpad, keyboard, and two big buttons, Stop and Take over.
  • This is the posture where Duo beats every other phone for Botkins. It's a tiny laptop pointed at the bot's machine.
  • Same screen flat? The viewer simply grows to fill; controls stay at the bottom.
Tent · standing on its edges, outer display out

Team board on StandBy

  • A StandBy widget, not an app screen. Big heads, one-word states, readable from across a desk.
  • No tap targets beyond "open". Needs-you count is the only number.
  • Reuses the widget target and Live Activity data the app already ships.

5 · Split View and the unfold moment

Open · 50/50 Split View

Botkins on the left, its rail on the left

  • In Split View each app puts its rail on its outer edge. Botkins on the left means a left rail, never one at the hinge.
  • At this width Botkins is compact again: one pane, same as closed. No special layout needed if safe areas are honored.
  • Content must respect the other app's rail too (asymmetric insets).

Continuity

Unfolding mid-conversation

  • The chat you were in stays selected and scrolled to the same message. The draft in the composer survives.
  • The list slides in from the leading edge. The rail does not move.
  • Folding back drops the list and returns to the exact chat. Never pop to the Chats tab.

6 · Recommendations and risks

Do

  • Ship the standard rail. Don't override placement. It's the platform's core pattern.
  • Add one hierarchy level when open: Chats|Chat, Work list|Task, Market|Detail, Settings|Detail.
  • Give every toolbar item a symbol and a title, and set visibility priorities.
  • Make Remote screen pose-aware: split viewer and controls at the fold in laptop pose.
  • Use even column counts for team grids and Today panels.

Don't

  • Build a separate "Duo layout". Size classes, safe areas and reserved regions cover every pose.
  • Put Send, the model picker chip or text buttons on the rail. They belong to the composer.
  • Keep Botkins' own "…" menu next to the system overflow. One ellipsis.
  • Rearrange controls when folding. Small shifts only.

Risks in our stack

  • Custom headers won't move. Chat's round Back and … buttons and Home's pill/+ row are drawn in content. Only native toolbar items reach the rail. These need to become Stack header items.
  • expo-router NativeTabs wraps UITabBarController, so it should get the vertical tab bar for free. Unverified on Expo 58 / RN 0.88: test in the Duo simulator first.
  • ReservedRegion, arrangement views and compression behavior are new UIKit/SwiftUI APIs with no React Native binding yet. A small native module is likely.

Assumption to check: the landscape rail edge on the outer display (drawn here on the camera side) and whether Live Activities expand down the rail exactly as drawn. Both come from Apple's written guidance, not a simulator run.
